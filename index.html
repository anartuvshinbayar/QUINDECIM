              In short

              What we measured

              Five things that shape life on the steppe: summer greenness, summer rain, summer and winter temperature, and the first autumn frost. For each of 337 soums, we tested 26 to 46 years of NASA satellite and model data.

              What we found

              Summers have really warmed in 197 of 337 soums since 1982, by about 2.0 °C in a typical one. Summer greenness has risen in 117 of 333 soums since 2001, mostly with wetter summers after the dry early 2000s. Summer rain has fallen in 11 soums and risen in 7; winters and the first frost show no clear trend yet.

              Why it matters

              Herders, schools and soum offices plan around pasture, water and frost. Knowing what is really changing in their own soum, and what is still just year-to-year noise, helps them plan. Each result says what it can and cannot tell: greener is not always healthier pasture, and a trend does not show its cause.

              How we test, and all the data

              Glossary

              Quindecim in brief

              Quindecim is a bilingual (English and Mongolian) web tool that answers one question for each of Mongolia's 337 soums and districts: is the climate really changing there, or is it just a few unusual years? It turns 26 to 46 years of NASA data into five questions and gives every place an honest verdict in one plain sentence. Built by Team Quindecim, Ulaanbaatar, for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, "Be An Earth System Trend Detective".

              What it found

              • In 117 of 333 soums, summer greenness has really risen since 2001.
              • In 11 of 337 soums, summer rainfall has really fallen since 1982.
              • In 197 of 337 soums, summers have really warmed since 1982, by about 2.0 °C in a typical one.
              • No soum shows a real change in winter temperature since 1983.
              • No soum shows a real change in the date of the first autumn frost since 1981.
              • So most of the greening since 2001 is the land recovering after a dry start, with wetter summers. Greener does not necessarily mean healthier pasture: NDVI measures how green the land looks from space, not forage quality, plant species or how many animals it can feed.

              How each verdict is made

              • One value per year per area, complete seasons only, at least 20 years.
              • Mann-Kendall trend test with the Hamed and Rao (1998) correction for year-to-year correlation; Sen's slope for the rate; a straight-line fit with an effective sample size (Santer et al. 2000) as a second opinion.
              • Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate across all areas: a real trend if q < 0.05, possible if p < 0.10, otherwise not proven.
              • Statistics checked against SciPy and an independent Python version (agreement within 0.0000001).
              • Checked against ten weather stations (NOAA GHCN-Daily). Summer: FLDAS rises and falls with the stations year by year (correlation 0.73 to 0.96, median 0.92) and, like them, warms at 10 of 10; but it warms less at 10 of 10 (FLDAS +0.2 to +0.5 °C per decade, stations +0.5 to +0.8), so the site's summer warming figures are cautious. Winter: FLDAS reads milder than the stations, by up to 12.6 °C in the cold western basins where winter inversions trap cold air (median +3.0 °C); FLDAS shows a clear winter trend at 0 of 10 stations and the stations themselves at 2, which matches the site's "not proven" for winters.

              Data

              • Summer greenness: MODIS MOD13A2 v061, June to August average, 2001–2026. Satellite pixels of 1 km (NASA MODIS), averaged over the grassland in each soum. Data confidence: high (measured from space every 16 days, the most direct of the five).
              • Summer rainfall: FLDAS Noah, monthly 0.1°, June to August total, 1982–2026. Land-model cells of 0.1° (about 11 × 8 km, NASA FLDAS); neighbouring soums often share values. Data confidence: lower (rainfall from a land model is the least certain of the five, so read its trends with care).
              • Summer temperature: FLDAS Noah, monthly 0.1°, June to August average, 1982–2026. Land-model cells of 0.1° (about 11 × 8 km, NASA FLDAS); neighbouring soums often share values. Data confidence: good (it rises and falls with ten weather stations year by year, but shows less warming than they measure, so its warming figures are cautious).
              • Winter temperature: FLDAS Noah, monthly 0.1°, December to February average, labelled by the January year, 1983–2026. Land-model cells of 0.1° (about 11 × 8 km, NASA FLDAS); neighbouring soums often share values. Data confidence: medium (the model misses the winter inversions of cold basins, so it reads milder than weather stations, by up to 13 °C in the Uvs basin; like most stations, it shows no clear winter trend).
              • First autumn frost: NASA POWER daily, T2M_MIN, First day after 1 July below 0 °C (day of year), 1981–2026. One NASA POWER grid cell (0.5° × 0.625°, about 55 × 47 km) at the soum centre; neighbouring soums often share it. Data confidence: medium (one coarse cell and a daily minimum below 0 °C give the general timing, not the exact frost day in one valley).
              • Areas: 328 soums and Ulaanbaatar's 9 districts, 337 in all, as drawn in the FAO GAUL 2025 boundaries (Mongolia officially counts 330 soums).

              Features

              • A map of the 21 aimags and Ulaanbaatar shaded by the direction and size of their trend; tap an aimag to see its soums, tap a soum for its chart and tests.
              • A summary of the whole country, with the largest real changes.
              • Share any result as a link or as an image for Facebook and Messenger; download the numbers as CSV.
              • Works on phones and slow connections; the page is usable before the data has arrived.

              Limits

              Straight-line trends only. Neighbouring areas share model cells, so their results are not independent. Rainfall from a land model is the least certain question. A trend is not a cause.

              Glossary

              Soum
              A district of a Mongolian province (aimag). This site uses 337 areas: 328 soums and Ulaanbaatar's 9 city districts, as drawn in the FAO GAUL 2025 boundaries; Mongolia officially counts 330 soums.
              Aimag
              One of Mongolia's 21 provinces. Ulaanbaatar, the capital, is counted with them here, making 22. An aimag's result uses the average of its soums.
              Time series
              The same measurement repeated every year, drawn as a line.
              Trend
              A steady movement up or down over many years.
              NDVI
              A satellite measure of how green the land is. Higher means more living plants.
              Mann-Kendall test
              Compares every year with every later year and counts ups against downs.
              Sen's slope
              The middle value of all the year-to-year slopes. The speed of the trend.
              t-test
              Checks whether the slope of a straight line through the points differs from zero.
              p value
              The chance of seeing a trend this strong if nothing were really changing.
              Significant
              p below 0.05. Unlikely to be chance.
              q value
              The p value adjusted for testing many places at once. Below 0.05 means the trend still holds up.
              Autocorrelation
              How much one year resembles the next. Runs of similar years can look like a trend, so the tests allow for it.
              Confidence interval
              The range the true rate of change most likely sits in.

              Data: NASA MODIS (MOD13A2 greenness, MCD12Q1 land cover), NASA FLDAS, NASA POWER. Soum boundaries: FAO GAUL 2025.
